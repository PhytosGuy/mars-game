Mars, the planet

The basic numbers of Mars that the whole game is built on.

Length of a day (a sol)NASA data

In the gameOne in-game day lasts 24.66 hours (24 h 39 min 35 s), and sets the day–night cycle, solar power and sleep.

Real worldA Martian solar day is 24 h 39 min 35 s. Mars turns once relative to the stars every 24 h 37 min.

NASA Mars Fact Sheet
Length of a yearNASA data

In the gameShown in the NASA facts.

Real worldMars takes 687 Earth days to orbit the Sun.

NASA Mars Fact Sheet
GravityNASA data

In the game3.72 m/s²: long, floaty jumps, low-traction walking, rover flips and the landing physics.

Real worldSurface gravity is 3.71 m/s², about 38% of Earth’s.

NASA Mars Fact Sheet
Air pressureNASA data

In the gameAbout 610 Pa, shown in the HUD; it rises and falls through the day and in storms. Spacesuits are needed outside.

Real worldAverage surface pressure is about 6.1 millibars (610 Pa), less than 1% of Earth’s at sea level.

NASA Mars Fact Sheet
What the air is made ofNASA data

In the gameThe Oxygen Unit splits carbon dioxide from the air; shown in the NASA facts.

Real worldAbout 95% carbon dioxide, 2.6–2.8% nitrogen and 1.9–2% argon, measured by Curiosity’s SAM instrument.

NASA Mars Fact SheetCuriosity
SunlightNASA data

In the gameSolar arrays produce less than half of what they would on Earth, and nothing at night.

Real worldMars receives about 586 W/m² of sunlight against Earth’s 1,361 W/m²: about 43%.

NASA Mars Fact SheetNASA Earth Fact Sheet
TemperaturesNASA data

In the gameEach landing site has its own range, from about −98 °C on cold nights to −10 °C on warm afternoons.

Real worldRover weather stations record nights near −90 °C and summer afternoons near 0 °C close to the equator.

Perseverance’s MEDA weather stationNASA Mars facts
RadiationNASA data

In the gameYour radiation dose outside is tracked; Regolith Shielding research lowers it.

Real worldCuriosity’s RAD instrument measured about 0.64 mSv per day on the surface; a round trip plus about 500 days on Mars adds up to roughly 1 sievert.

Curiosity
Radio delayNASA data

In the gameWhy the landing flies itself and why the colony can’t get live help from Earth.

Real worldLight takes about 3 to 22 minutes to travel between Earth and Mars, depending on where the planets are.

NASA Mars facts
The moons Phobos and DeimosNASA data

In the gamePhobos crosses the night sky, rising in the west.

Real worldPhobos orbits Mars about three times a day, faster than Mars turns, so it rises in the west and sets in the east. It is slowly spiralling inward.

NASA: Phobos
Olympus Mons and Valles MarinerisNASA data

In the gameShown in the NASA facts.

Real worldOlympus Mons is about 22 km tall, roughly two and a half times Everest; Valles Marineris is a canyon system more than 4,000 km long and up to 7 km deep.

Olympus Mons (NASA photo archive)NASA Mars facts
The colour of the skyNASA data

In the gameThe day sky is butterscotch, and the sky around the setting Sun turns blue.

Real worldFine dust makes the daytime sky butterscotch and scatters blue light near the Sun at sunset, as Curiosity photographed in Gale Crater in 2015.

Sunset in Gale Crater (PIA19400)
CloudsNASA data

In the gameWispy clouds drift across the sky.

Real worldCuriosity and Perseverance have photographed thin water-ice clouds high above Mars.

CuriosityPerseverance
SoundNASA data

In the gameShown in the NASA facts.

Real worldPerseverance’s microphones showed sound travels at about 240 m/s on Mars and fades quickly in the thin air.

The five landing sites

Each site is generated by the game in the spirit of a real place. The terrain does not reproduce real elevation data.

Jezero-type DeltaNASA data

In the gameAn ancient river delta with layered, terraced ground and a crater rim.

Real worldJezero Crater (18.4°N, 77.5°E) held a lake about 3.5 billion years ago; Perseverance landed there on 18 February 2021 to explore its delta.

Perseverance
Ice-rich PlainsGame design

In the gameA flat ice sheet with blue ice-crystal mountains, and ice signatures nearby.

Real worldInspired by Utopia and Arcadia Planitia, where the SHARAD radar on the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter found thick buried ice. The crystal mountains are a game design: real Martian ice is buried under the soil.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter
Crater BasinGame design

In the gameYour base sits on the floor of a large crater with walls all around and a small layered mound.

Real worldInspired by Gale Crater and Mount Sharp (Aeolis Mons), about 5 km tall, which Curiosity has been climbing since 2014. The game’s crater is smaller than the real one.

Curiosity
Volcanic HighlandsGame design

In the gameA small plain ringed by four large volcanoes under a red-tinted sky, with lava-tube caves.

Real worldInspired by the Tharsis region and the lava-tube skylights orbiters have seen on Arsia Mons. The red sky tint is a game choice.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (HiRISE)
Rocky PlainsGame design

In the gameA flat plain ringed by pointed mountains.

Real worldInspired by Elysium Planitia, the flat plain where the InSight lander studied marsquakes. The ring of peaks is a game design.

InSight

Entry, descent and landing

The landing sequence follows the real steps of a Mars landing.

Entry speed and heatNASA data

In the gameThe capsule enters at about 20,000 km/h and the heat shield glows.

Real worldPerseverance hit the top of the atmosphere at about 20,000 km/h; its heat shield reached about 1,300 °C.

Perseverance landing timeline
A landing that flies itselfNASA data

In the gameEntry, parachute and heat-shield release are automatic; you take over for the powered descent.

Real worldBecause of the radio delay, every Mars landing is flown by the spacecraft’s own computer: NASA calls it the “seven minutes of terror”.

Perseverance landing timeline
Supersonic parachuteNASA data

In the gameThe parachute opens high up while you are still very fast.

Real worldPerseverance’s 21.5 m parachute opened about 11 km up at around 1,500 km/h.

Perseverance landing timeline
Terrain-Relative Navigation and LIDARNASA data

In the gameAfter the heat shield drops, the LIDAR screen scans the ground below.

Real worldPerseverance compared camera images with orbital maps to steer to a safe spot (Terrain-Relative Navigation).

Perseverance landing timeline
Touchdown below 3 m/sGame estimate

In the gameLand slower than 3 m/s for a good landing; your fuel is limited.

Real worldA game target. Real landers such as Perseverance’s sky crane lowered the rover at about 0.75 m/s.

Perseverance landing timeline

Life support and the colony

How the colony’s oxygen, water, food and power are worked out.

How much a crew needsNASA data

In the gameA crew of four uses about 3.4 kg of oxygen, 16 L of water and 7.2 kg of food per sol.

Real worldNASA’s life-support planning uses about 0.84 kg of oxygen and about 1.8 kg of food per person per day; the game rounds these for four people.

NASA Life Support Baseline Values
Oxygen Unit (MOXIE)Game estimate

In the game+6 kg of oxygen per sol from carbon dioxide in the air, using 1.5 kW.

Real worldPerseverance’s MOXIE made 122 g of oxygen in total at up to about 12 g per hour. The game’s unit is a scaled-up version, as NASA plans for future crews.

NASA: MOXIE completes its mission
Ice DrillGame estimate

In the game+39 L of water per sol, only on ice-rich ground.

Real worldA game estimate. Buried mid-latitude ice is real and was mapped by MRO’s SHARAD radar.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter
GreenhouseGame estimate

In the game+5 kg of food per sol from LED hydroponics, using 7 L of water.

Real worldA game estimate based on the idea of growing food under lights, as astronauts do on the International Space Station.

International Space Station
Water recoveryNASA data

In the gameThe Water Recovery research halves the crew’s water use.

Real worldThe ISS recycles about 98% of its water.

NASA: ISS water recovery milestone
Solar arrays and batteriesGame estimate

In the game2.5 kW per array at noon, nothing at night; 30 kWh per battery bank.

Real worldGame estimates. They follow the real rule that Mars gets about 43% of Earth’s sunlight and that dust lowers output.

NASA Mars Fact Sheet
Fission Power Unit (Kilopower)NASA data

In the game10 kW day and night, unaffected by dust or darkness.

Real worldNASA’s Kilopower project tested a small fission reactor (KRUSTY) in 2018, for designs from 1 to 10 kW.

KRUSTY test results (NASA NTRS)
PerchloratesNASA data

In the gameA hazard in soil and water that must be treated; a discovery that raises greenhouse yields.

Real worldThe Phoenix lander found toxic perchlorate salts in the soil in 2008.

Phoenix
Dust on solar panelsNASA data

In the gameDust builds up on arrays and lowers power until cleaned; dust devils sometimes clean them.

Real worldDust ended the Opportunity and InSight missions, and dust devils cleaned the panels of Spirit and Opportunity.

Hazards and geology

The things on Mars that push back.

Dust stormsNASA data

In the gameStorms build up, darken the sky, cover solar arrays and bury equipment.

Real worldA planet-wide dust storm in 2018 blocked out the Sun and ended Opportunity’s mission.

Opportunity
MarsquakesNASA data

In the gameQuakes open fissures and can seal caves; the Seismometer warns you first.

Real worldInSight recorded over 1,300 marsquakes; the largest, in May 2022, was about magnitude 4.7. Seismometers feel the fast P-waves before the stronger shaking.

InSight
Meteor impactsNASA data

In the gameImpacts leave fresh craters and expose ore.

Real worldOrbiters such as the Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter regularly spot new impact craters on Mars.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter
Lava tubes and cavesNASA data

In the gameSinkholes lead down into lava-tube caves you can explore.

Real worldOrbiters have spotted dark “skylight” pits on Arsia Mons, likely openings into lava tubes that could shelter future explorers from radiation and temperature swings.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (HiRISE)
Buried iceNASA data

In the gameIce signatures and the ice biome; the water mission sends you there.

Real worldMRO’s SHARAD radar mapped thick sheets of buried ice at mid-latitudes, and Phoenix watched bright ice under the soil sublimate away within days.

Mars Reconnaissance OrbiterPhoenix
Ice biome with crystal mountainsGame design

In the gameA frozen plain with climbable mountains of blue ice crystal.

Real worldA game design that makes buried ice something you can see and reach. Real Martian ice is mostly under the surface or in the polar caps.

Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter

Planet positions and launch windowsCalculated

In the gameLaunch windows are found from the real positions of Earth and Mars for any date.

Real worldThe lab uses JPL’s “Approximate Positions of the Planets” (mean longitudes, valid 1800–2050).

JPL: Approximate Positions of the Planets
Hohmann transfer to MarsCalculated

In the gameTransfer time 259 days; Mars must lead Earth by 44.3°; windows every 780 days (about 26 months); Δv 2.94 km/s to leave and 2.64 km/s to arrive; launch energy C3 ≈ 8.7 km²/s².

Real worldCalculated in the game with Kepler’s third law and the vis-viva equation, using orbit sizes and speeds from NASA fact sheets. Real missions fly slightly different paths (Perseverance took 203 days).
